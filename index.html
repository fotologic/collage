About this app

Photocollage has a long history, as old as the medium itself. The Victorians were fond of cutting up photographs for their family albums. In the early 20th century, Dadaists and Surrealists experimented with collage and montage to disrupt the authority of the photograph — for formal, political and psychological reasons that still feel urgent. These experiments continued through the modern period and are now a staple of postmodern and conceptual practice. Some contemporary photographers have returned to the materiality of the print and related ephemera, perhaps as a way to resist the increasingly digital and virtual nature of image culture.

This app is a way to begin playing with scraps of visual information — scanned paper fragments and photos of photos from books. You can construct a photocollage entirely from these fragments, or upload your own images. You can intervene directly in a photograph: split it, punch a hole through it. You can layer, scale and reposition everything by hand. When you're done, export the result. Or print it and continue with real scissors and glue.

The most important thing is intuition. The app produces starting points, not finished objects — something to look at, respond to, disrupt further. If a composition feels wrong, it probably is. If it feels right, trust that. And if you print the result and take scissors to it, so much the better.
